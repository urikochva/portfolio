ThirdWave

An online coffee store designed to be easy to navigate on any device.

ThirdWave is a UX/UI case study focused on intuitive digital navigation and multi-device adaptability. It follows the process from wireframes and user flows to a polished, responsive interface that works across desktop, tablet and mobile.

The problem and the solution

Hassle of going out
Getting quality coffee means traffic, parking, queues and a premium price per cup. Solution: freshly roasted beans delivered to your home or office, with fast delivery and reliable subscriptions.
Guessing the flavor
Specialty beans are a gamble: too acidic, too bitter, or just not your taste. Solution: a short interactive taste quiz and clear flavor notes, such as chocolate or berries, that lead to the right beans.
Lack of options
Outside a few neighborhoods, third-wave coffee and professional brewing gear are hard to find in Israel. Solution: a one-stop shop for high-end beans and brewing equipment, available anywhere in the country.

Research

Competitors
The research reviewed leading coffee websites in Israel and abroad, among them welovecoffee.co.il, rooster-coffee.co.il and quick-mill.com, and followed Facebook groups for coffee enthusiasts to understand what drives their purchases.
Key findings
Standard sites felt cluttered, so the design uses generous whitespace for a calm, premium layout. Competitors rely on basic filters, so users are guided with tasting notes and origin stories. Typography is elegant and editorial, and instead of bulk deals there are curated “tasting trios”.
Questionnaire: habits
53% drink fresh coffee beans, 18% capsules, 15% instant coffee and 12% black coffee.
Questionnaire: buying
50% buy online from roasteries they know, 37% buy in person at a local roastery, and 12% order online from roasteries abroad.
Questionnaire: trust
Two things make people most comfortable ordering beans online: a refund or free replacement (53.8%) and detailed reviews and ratings from other customers (53.8%). A low-price tasting kit and a short matching questionnaire each scored 50%.

User persona

Who
Idan, 28, from Tel Aviv. A project manager at a tech startup who loves aesthetics and quality coffee. His interest started on a trip to South America, and he has since read widely, joined roasting workshops and built a collection of equipment.
What drives him
Quality and professionalism, clean design, and a drive for perfection in everything he does.
Goals
Find unique, freshly roasted beans, buy professional brewing equipment for home, and one day visit the countries where coffee is grown.
Pain points
Coffee websites without detailed information about the beans, and the difficulty of finding specialized brewing equipment in physical stores in Israel.
In his words
“Perfect coffee doesn’t happen by accident. It’s the result of technique, strict measurement, and absolute control over every variable.”

Designing for usability

Efficiency
Intuitive filters and a simplified single-page checkout keep the path to purchase to the minimum number of clicks.
Time to understand
Clear, relatable language replaces coffee jargon. A bold value proposition on the homepage and the interactive taste quiz guide new users to the right product in seconds.
Time to reorder
A dedicated dashboard for repeat customers has a one-click “Restock” button. Saved shipping and payment details make reordering a 30-second task.
